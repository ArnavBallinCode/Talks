Two ways to interpret

People where you have them.
AI where you don't.

Human interpreters

The booth, in a browser

  • Professional interpreters, on site or remote
  • Active and backup per language, swapping every 20–30 min
  • Booth chat and a live floor monitor
  • Sub-second audio to every listener
AI interpretation

Speech → text → speech

  • Live captions of the floor
  • Translated captions in many languages
  • A synthetic voice reading the translation
  • Tuned per room with a persona, style and glossary
  • Both run in the same room at once: German from a human booth, Japanese from the AI.
  • Frame AI as coverage, not replacement.

The hard parts

What breaks when it's live.

1

One voice per channel

Two interpreters live at once means listeners hear both. MediaMTX replaces the old publisher, and the booth registry decides who's active.

30 s

Tickets, not open mics

Going live redeems a single-use ticket. It expires in 30 seconds and only opens that booth's path, for the active interpreter.

0

Roles from the client

The browser never says who it is. Roles come from the server session, so a modified client can't promote itself.

  • Pick two if you're running long. Handoff is the one people remember.
